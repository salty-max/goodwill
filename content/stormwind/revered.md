title: The First Bandage

My mother sewed for the Cathedral when I was small. She took me with her, and I learned to roll a bandage before I could write my own name. Tight at the start, loose at the end, never let it twist. My hands still do it when I am nervous, with whatever is on the desk. I have rolled up council minutes that way, and nobody missed them.

She never ran anything. She worked at the end of a long table with a basket and did the work, and went home, and came back the next day. I run the effort. I write the forms and keep the ledgers and stand up at meetings. Some nights I think she would find all that very funny.

I run this desk the way she rolled bandages. Tight at the start, loose at the end, never let it twist. I am not sure I would have taken the post at all, if not for her. I am very glad I did, most of all on the days somebody comes through that door with their arms full.
