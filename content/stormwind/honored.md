Friend! Come and meet the effort. The real one, I mean, not the ledgers.

Every evening, in a back room near the Cathedral, the volunteers come in to sew. Widows of the guard. Apprentices from the tailors' shops, who are paid nothing and argue about seams as if they were. A retired sergeant who rolls bandages with one hand and tells the same old stories about Redridge. Everyone pretends not to have heard them.

I keep their schedule. I keep their thread. I keep the peace between the widow who hems from the left and the apprentice who hems from the right. It is the hardest diplomacy in this city, whatever the council thinks.

[after: 7791] Your cloth comes through their hands. They talk about you, by the way. "The big donor", the sergeant calls you. I have tried to correct him to "our valued contributor". He refuses on principle. Your wool has been through more arguments in that room than the council has had all year.
