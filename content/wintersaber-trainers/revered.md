When a cub first comes to me, it will not eat from my hand. It takes the food from the snow once I have stepped back, and it hisses while it eats. I put the meat down every day, a little closer. One day it takes the meat from my fingers. I have never been able to tell which day it will be. I have only learned to keep putting the meat down.

It came to me last night that I have been doing the same with you. The meat, the same errand over and over, the furbolgs, the giants. A little closer each time. I did not plan it. I know no other way to teach anything.

The sabers know your scent now. When you come up the path, the young ones lift their heads and lay them down again. From a saber, that is a great courtesy.

I should have asked whether you minded. You did not leave. With a cub, that is the answer, and I have taken it as yours.
