The Outriders don't ask who you are any more, not even the new ones. I'd say you earned that, but it's a small word for it.

One day I mean to open this counter on a morning when nobody comes in hurt, and sell something that isn't for killing. Lamps. Good boots for walking, not running. Fishing line. Toys for the clan's young ones, the kind that only make a noise. A cradle or two, carved from wood nobody had to fight for. I've never had a morning like that.

I've sold rations and bandages and repairs to the Outriders for so long that I've forgotten what it's like to hand somebody a thing and not wonder whether they'll be alive to use it up.

I won't get my wish soon. I know that. The gulch isn't going anywhere, and neither are the elves, and neither are we. Most mornings start with somebody carried in off the field, and the first thing I sell is linen.

When it comes, it'll be orcs wanting boots, and nobody waiting on linen. I don't know what I'll say to them. Something about the boots, probably.
