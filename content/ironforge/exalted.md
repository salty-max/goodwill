A lad came down off the high ridge last week. Half frozen, beard full of ice, sent down with a cough the post couldn't mend. He stood at this desk dripping on my ledger and told me the socks were the only warm thing on that ridge all winter, and that every dwarf up there had a pair, and that he'd come to say so himself because nobody else would bother.

Those socks were your wool. I checked the ledger, and then checked it again, to be sure. He's a good lad.

So I did a thing I've been not doing for years. I took the old shield down from behind the door, and I gave it to him, for when he goes back up. It's a good shield. It was wasted on a wall. I'll not need it again. I've got this desk, and this desk has more dwarves behind it than any shield ever did.

Steel wins the day. Wool wins the winter. I learned that one slowly, and you helped me learn it, bale by bale, without ever being told you were teaching.

Now, the lad's waiting for his socks. Pass me that crate.
