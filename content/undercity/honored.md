Before, I sold silk.

In Lordaeron. The city above us, when it was a city. I had a shop off the square, and the court bought from me, and I knew the weight of every bolt I sold and the name of every lady who wore it. I was good at it. I was proud of being good at it. That, I am told, is a very living thing to be.

Then the plague came, and then the rest. I came to myself in the ruins above, with the same hands and none of the same customers, and later came down here. The first thing they gave me to do was count bandages. The coarsest wool, for holding dead soldiers together.

I could have refused. I knew some who did, in those first years. I did not. It turned out a merchant who knows cloth is useful to the dead as well. The irony is instructive.
