Tonight we are here. By the next moon we will be gone.

That is how the Faire lives. We set up in the grass of Elwynn, under the oaks, and play for the farmers and the soldiers and the children who ran away from their chores. Then we fold every tent, pack every crate, coax every beast into its wagon, and roll off to Mulgore, where the tauren come down from the bluffs to find out what the noise is about.

A fair that stays in one place becomes a market, and a market is a dull thing. We never stay. Some of my folk were born on the road and have never spent a whole season in one field. They would not know what to do with a roof.

The wagons know the way better than I do by now. Elwynn, then Mulgore, then Elwynn again, and every time we come back, the same children are a little taller and pretend they have outgrown us.
