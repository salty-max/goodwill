One of my sisters fell at the far end of the gulch last spring. She went for their banner alone, because she was fast and young and certain. She was fast. She was young. She was wrong. They had waited for exactly that, behind the wall of their mill, and they did not need to be fast.

I carried her glaive home. It hangs in my tent. I clean it once a moon. It does not need it. The edge is still nicked where she parried the first axe, and I have never filed it out. Nobody asks whose it is. Sentinels do not ask each other that. Her name I keep. That is what we do with each other's names, when the rest is gone.

You have been in that gulch long enough to know what it costs to go after their banner. I tried to teach her patience, the whole of one summer. She was faster than my teaching.
