The orcs at Hammerfall made warding signs when I first came, the old kind, when they thought I wasn't looking. I didn't mind. I've been warded against by better.

Then a sergeant of theirs, a big scarred cheerful brute with half an ear, took to bringing me the first news of the day before anyone else had it. Who held the mine. Who'd lost the stables. Who'd fallen, and whether they'd get up again. As if I might want it first. I did.

The Defilers are no family. Half the orcs think I smell, and they're right. But when one of ours goes down at the smithy, it's orcs and trolls and tauren and Forsaken who go back for them together, under the same arrows, and nobody stops to ask what anyone else is.

Last night the sergeant took the long watch on the gate, so that I wouldn't have to explain to the new recruits why I don't sleep. Nobody asked him to. He told them I was at my prayers. I let him.
